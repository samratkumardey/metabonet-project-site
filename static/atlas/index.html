Abstract. The MetaboNet type 1 diabetes corpus pools 14 clinical studies into a single 37-column schema covering 154,842,077 five-minute epochs from 1,492 subject records. Aggregate profiling establishes what the corpus contains but cannot establish whether any individual record is coherent. This atlas renders the raw record — no imputation, no unit conversion, no derived classification — for three deterministically selected patients from every level of three stratifying dimensions, in the training and evaluation splits separately. Every figure is interactive: each point reports its own timestamp, source column, value and unit. A companion set of figures profiles what data each insulin delivery device actually generates. Section 5 documents what the corpus does not contain for glucose, insulin and food, which bounds what any figure here, or any model trained on this data, can represent.

2   Method

Stratification. The largest contributing study accounts for the majority of all rows, so a random sample of patients is in practice a sample of that study. Selection is therefore stratified over three dimensions — contributing study (source_file), CGM sensor model (cgm_device) and insulin delivery device (insulin_delivery_device) — with 3 patients drawn from every level, in the training and evaluation splits separately.

Selection rule. Subjects with at least 864 CGM readings are ordered by MD5(seed | source_file | id) and the first 3 taken. The ordering is independent of row order, file order and thread count, so the sample is reproducible. The day rendered is the subject's best-covered calendar day (at least 180 of a possible 288 readings), preferring days that also carry bolus and carbohydrate records.

Subject key. Subjects are keyed on (split, source_file, id) rather than id alone, because a subject may appear in both splits and identifier strings are not guaranteed unique across contributing studies.

Raw values only. Every plotted series is a column of the parquet file at the value stored there. No imputation, no unit conversion, no derived classification. Summary statistics appear only in tables, prefixed computed_, and are never drawn into a figure.

5   Figure catalogue — consecutive-day records

One or two patients per split drawn across 2 consecutive calendar days in a single continuous panel, with the midnight boundary marked by a long dashed line. A day is the unit a CGM report is built from, but it is an arbitrary cut: the overnight period, when nothing is eaten and nothing is announced, is the cleanest readout of basal adequacy in the whole record and it sits exactly on that cut. These panels show it whole, and show whether a patient's pattern repeats from one day to the next.

7   Limitations — what this corpus does not contain

Pooling fourteen studies into one schema necessarily keeps only what all of them share. The following are standard fields in diabetes device downloads that are not columns in this dataset, and therefore cannot appear in any figure here nor be used by any model trained on it.

Glucose

  • Sensor session boundaries, warm-up periods and calibration events
  • Reference fingerstick blood glucose (SMBG)
  • Sensor serial, wear site, firmware revision, or any per-reading quality flag
  • The trend arrow presented to the patient at the time of a decision

Insulin

  • Bolus type — normal, extended, dual-wave and automated correction boluses are stored identically as a single number
  • Programmed basal rate profile, temporary basal, and pump suspend events
  • Insulin on board, carbohydrates on board, and every bolus-calculator setting (carb ratio, correction factor, active insulin time, target)
  • Automated-mode entry and exit events; the algorithm field is constant per subject
  • Infusion set and site changes; non-insulin glucose-lowering medication

Food

  • Anything beyond grams of carbohydrate — no fat, protein, fibre, glycemic index or portion
  • Whether an entry was announced before the meal or logged retrospectively
  • Any structured food coding; meal_label is sparse free text

Context

  • Timezone and daylight-saving information; timestamps are naive
  • Sleep, illness, stress, menstrual cycle and alcohol
  • Exercise in practice — the workout_* columns exist but are essentially empty
  • A per-row units declaration; mg/dL is asserted rather than recorded

Columns that exist in the schema but are empty or near-empty in the evaluation split are listed in tables/61_column_coverage.csv and plotted in 00_channel_availability.html.
