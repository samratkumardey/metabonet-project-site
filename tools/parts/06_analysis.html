Analysis

What the results say

RQ1 — Information moves accuracy; architecture does not

In the tabular pipeline we measured nine ideas one at a time against the same baseline. One of them — adding the insulin and carbohydrate recorded in the next two hours — was worth +4.52 DTS-A at 30 minutes (88.05 → 92.57). The other eight together were worth +0.78, and two were negative.

On the deep side we ran seven paired single-change screens against the plain LSTM — same anchors, folds, seed, loss and budget, one change each — on all five held-out studies. None improved it.

Reading. For this task the content of the upcoming insulin and carbohydrate schedule carries the signal; its minute-level ordering and the choice of sequence encoder do not. Architecture is a controlled variable here, not the lever.

RQ2 — "Unseen" is two different questions

Our two test sets disagreed. On whole held-out studies the 50/50 blend beat the pure LSTM on four of five (Figure 7). On the public test set the pure LSTM beat the blend at every horizon, every paired interval clear of zero. The leading explanation — that the public set shares patients with training and a sequence model recognises them — does not survive: split the public set by whether a patient also appears in training, and the LSTM wins on both groups, including the 151 patients it has never seen.

Reading. A blend that hedges with LightGBM wins at reaching a new study; the sequence model wins at reaching new patients from a known study. The annual set is the second kind: none of its 92 subject ids appears in training, but its insulin-delivery algorithm string (iLet Bionic Pancreas) occurs in exactly one training study. Our pre-registered rule was written for the first kind; we report both and say which entry each favours (Submissions).
The Loop row is confounded. Loop is 73% of training data, so holding it out leaves a quarter of the usual training set. "LightGBM suits Loop" and "LightGBM degrades more gracefully when data is scarce" fit equally well and have opposite implications. The annual model trains on all 7.09 M anchors, so if the second is true it does not apply. Untested.

RQ3 — Offline evaluation can be trusted, and the model is calibrated

Scoring. On all five entries checked, the leaderboard's per-horizon DTS-A, MARD and RMSE equal our offline values to the board's rounding (table). A noise floor of ~1.8 points we had assumed between local and board scores was imaginary; paired subject-bootstrap intervals are the right test, and effects of a few tenths are measurable.

Calibration. An earlier table binned errors by the true glucose and showed predictions too high when truth was low and too low when truth was high; we read that as shrinkage and tried three post-hoc corrections, all of which failed. Binning by the truth produces exactly that pattern for a perfectly calibrated predictor — it is regression to the mean, and nothing applied at prediction time can repair it. The actionable question bins by what the model predicted:

Mechanism. The +1.80 mg/dL at 120 minutes is the gap between the mean and the median of a right-skewed distribution, and it widens with horizon as uncertainty grows. L1 on the log ratio targets the median. Shifting toward the mean helps RMSE (mean-optimal) and hurts MARD and zone A (both median-optimal). This closes global shift, band-centred point selection and de-shrinkage as one family with one reason, and independently confirms the choice of objective.
